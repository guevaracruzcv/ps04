Title: From AI pilots to public value: build capacity, test carefully and keep people accountable

Evidence as of: 2026-10-13. Historical V4 as-of date: 2026-10-11.

Predecessor: editorial/human_review_m19/briefs_v1/consolidated_briefs_v1.jsonl

Research question: What should governments do next to use AI responsibly and sustainably in public services?

Argument: Governments should treat AI as an institutional capability to build and maintain, not a tool to deploy by default. A practical approach begins with a defined public problem, tests whether AI adds value, establishes fit-for-purpose data and procurement safeguards, equips staff, and maintains human responsibility for consequential decisions.

Synthesis: The World Bank proposes a sequence for moving from pilots to sustained public-service use: match AI to a problem, prepare data, evaluate pilots, adapt procurement, and invest in capacity over time. MIT’s transit project illustrates a related design principle: integrate information to support staff rather than assuming that operational decisions should be automated. The Sigma webinar emphasizes translating formal principles into practical guidance and communicating that guidance through repeated messages, training and other organizational channels. These sources support a cautious direction: governments can explore AI, but should connect experiments to evidence, institutional ownership, staff capability and safeguards. They do not show that one procurement or governance model will fit every jurisdiction.

Limitations: The World Bank examples and figures are attributed to its report and do not establish that the same results will occur elsewhere. PTIQ’s stated aims are not demonstrated outcomes. Sigma webinar advice is presenter guidance, not a binding rule for all governments; requirements depend on applicable law and local policy. This brief identifies considerations for decision-makers, not legal advice or a claim that any named jurisdiction has implemented all of them.
