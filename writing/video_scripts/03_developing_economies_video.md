## Script and shot list

**[Opening - Presenter on camera]**

Artificial intelligence offers opportunities to developing economies. But access to powerful technology does not automatically create economic development.

Why?

**[On-screen graphic: Technology + Skills + Infrastructure + Institutions]**

The World Bank emphasizes that AI adoption depends on complementary capabilities: infrastructure, foundational skills, and effective institutions.

A government can purchase an advanced system without having the capacity to operate it sustainably. A successful pilot does not automatically become a reliable public service.

**[On-screen: Innovation beyond invention]**

In an IMF podcast, Mehran Gul discusses why innovation should not be understood only through the world's largest technology companies.

For developing economies, adapting existing technologies to solve real problems may be an important opportunity. But adaptation requires people and institutions with appropriate capabilities.

The FCT&L webinar examines essential skills in an AI-enhanced world, while SIGMA highlights the importance of preparing civil servants.

**[On-screen: Public value, not just adoption]**

For Cabo Verde, these insights raise useful questions. Which public-service problems should AI help solve? Do institutions have the skills, data, and infrastructure to operate these tools? How can governments avoid unnecessary dependence on individual suppliers?

**[Closing - Presenter on camera]**

The development opportunity is not simply to acquire AI. It is to build the capacity to use AI responsibly, effectively, and sustainably.

That is how technological promise can become public value.
