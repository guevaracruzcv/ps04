## Script and shot list

**[Opening - Presenter on camera]**

Will artificial intelligence take our jobs?

That is one of the biggest questions in today's technology debate. But it is not the only question we should be asking.

**[On-screen graphic: Jobs > Tasks > Skills > Opportunity]**

A recent Harvard Gazette article explores why AI has not yet triggered the wave of mass layoffs that some predicted. That does not mean workers face no risks. It means the relationship between automation and employment is more complex.

Jobs consist of tasks, responsibilities, relationships, and human judgment. AI may transform those elements without eliminating an entire occupation.

**[On-screen: Worker participation]**

MIT Sloan's examination of Kaiser Permanente's AI labor agreement adds another lesson: workers need a voice in technological change.

The Reuters podcast *AI at Work* highlights another issue: productivity gains do not automatically become better wages or opportunities for employees.

And the FCT&L discussion of essential skills reminds us that education must prepare people to evaluate, adapt, and work alongside AI.

**[Closing - Presenter on camera]**

For governments and public institutions, the challenge is not simply adopting more technology. It is helping people develop capabilities to use it, while examining who benefits from innovation.

The future of work is also a question of governance.

Next in the series: Who should decide, humans or AI?
