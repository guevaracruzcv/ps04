## Script and shot list

**[Opening - Presenter on camera]**

Artificial intelligence can help public institutions make decisions faster. But should it make those decisions independently?

Consider two situations.

An AI system identifies missing documents in an administrative application. Another recommends whether someone qualifies for an important public benefit.

These are not equivalent decisions.

**[On-screen graphic: Risk x Ambiguity]**

MIT Sloan describes a framework that considers how risky and ambiguous a decision is. Routine, low-risk tasks may be suitable for automation. Complex decisions with serious consequences require stronger human authority.

**[On-screen: Civil servants and accountability]**

The SIGMA webinar on AI use by civil servants reminds us that public employees need practical knowledge and appropriate training.

Human oversight must mean more than clicking an approval button. Employees need enough information and authority to question or correct AI recommendations.

The World Bank also emphasizes that moving from AI pilots to sustainable public services requires procurement expertise, interoperability, auditability, and institutional capacity.

**[Closing - Presenter on camera]**

For countries such as Cabo Verde, the opportunity is to explore how AI can improve public services without weakening accountability.

AI may support decisions. Public institutions must remain responsible for the authority they exercise.

Next in the series: How can developing economies turn AI opportunities into public value?
