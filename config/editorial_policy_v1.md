# PS04 Editorial Policy — Version 1

## Mission
Publication: AI & Public-Sector Capability

Central question:
Who benefits from AI in public administration,
who risks being left behind, and how should
governments respond?

Audience: policymakers, public managers, researchers.

## Evidence eligibility
- Publication must fall within the 30-day UTC run window.
- The original publisher and publication date must be known.
- Substantive text or timed transcript must be preserved.
- Evidence must have an item_id and version_id.
- Duplicates and syndicated copies are not independent sources.
- Test fixtures must never enter the editorial corpus.

## Editorial selection
AI recommends one decision per item:

ACCEPT — credible, relevant and substantively useful.
REJECT — irrelevant, duplicated or ineligible.
DEFER — promising but requiring verification.

Every recommendation must explain its evidence and reasoning.

## Human editorial authority
The human editor reviews and confirms the final decisions.
At least three accepted and three rejected/deferred
recommendations must be documented.

AI cannot automatically approve final publication.

## Claim verification
Consequential claims must reference:
- item_id and version_id
- supporting passage or timestamp
- original publisher and URL

Distinguish findings, opinions and uncertainty.
Include credible counterarguments.

## Three-part editorial series
Article 1 — What changed and why does it matter?
Article 2 — Which claims withstand scrutiny?
Article 3 — What should governments do next?

Each article requires at least three independent sources.

## Revision and corrections
Record any policy change and its justification.
Rerun the affected editorial stage after revision.
Preserve earlier decisions and corrections.

## Publication
Human approval is required before publication.
